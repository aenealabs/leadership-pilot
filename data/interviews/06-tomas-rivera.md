Listening tour, week 4. Tomas Rivera, Export Compliance Manager, Trade Compliance. 30 minutes.

Tomas confirmed the two premature-share incidents Marcus mentioned. Neither became a violation, "but we were lucky, not controlled." The root cause in both: drawings in PLM had no export classification, so nothing stopped them from being shared.

"Classification is optional in PLM today. It should be impossible to release a controlled item without it." Tomas estimates thousands of legacy items from the acquisitions have no classification at all.

Tomas wants access to controlled data driven by classification and by each person's authorization, not by folder permissions someone set up years ago. Quarterly access attestations are done by hand in spreadsheets and take two weeks.

On suppliers: Trade Compliance owns the export licenses, but supplier license data lives with Procurement, "and the two are never in sync." This answers Daniel's open question only partly; nobody owns keeping them aligned.

Tomas is wary of AI tools touching controlled technical data. "Whatever you build, I need to know where the data goes and who can see it."

Success: no controlled item released without classification, and access attestation in days, not weeks.

Follow-up: share the classification backlog count by business unit.
